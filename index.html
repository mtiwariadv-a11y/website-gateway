Academic Section & Legal Write-Ups

By Manoj Kumar Tiwari, Advocate

An in-depth analysis parsing the intersection between domestic constitutional protections under Article 21 of the Constitution of India and customary international obligations regarding refugee protection. In the absence of explicit, specific domestic refugee legislation within the sovereign boundaries of India, higher judicial organs have increasingly leveraged custom state tracking principles to enforce human rights safety nets.

The customary international principle of non-refoulement protects an individual from being forcefully expelled or returned to a territory where their life or baseline liberties face existential threats. This commentary charts the historical evolution of appellate litigation handling refugee mandates and looks closely at how higher court benches maintain strict compliance bounds aligned with multilateral treaty expectations while preserving domestic internal security vectors.
